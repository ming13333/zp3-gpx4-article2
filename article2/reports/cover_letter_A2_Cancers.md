# Cover Letter — Cancers (MDPI)

**Date:** 17 August 2026

**To:** The Editorial Office of Cancers, MDPI

Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "ZP3 Expression Shows No Consistent Association with Immune Checkpoint Blockade Response in Three Public Cohorts: An Association-Level Evaluation of Bulk Transcript Abundance" for consideration as an Article. The study asks whether bulk-tumor ZP3 transcript abundance, a candidate gene linked to immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments, is associated with response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB), and answers this question with a pre-specified analysis of three public ICB-treated cohorts spanning two tumor types.

Single-gene predictive markers for ICB frequently fail to replicate across cohorts, yet negative findings remain underreported, which inflates the apparent reliability of candidate markers. For ZP3, evidence is currently limited to individual cohorts, and no multi-cohort evaluation of its predictive value has been reported. This gap matters because ZP3 has entered the immuno-oncology discussion through a proposed extracellular GPX4-ZP3 axis, and claims of clinical utility should rest on reproducible evidence.

The manuscript contributes in three ways. First, it applies pre-specified, cohort-level tests to three independent datasets (GSE78220, melanoma, anti-PD-1, n=27; IMvigor210, urothelial carcinoma, anti-PD-L1, n=298 evaluable; GSE91061, melanoma, anti-PD-1, n=49 pre-treatment samples), reporting effect sizes and 95% confidence intervals alongside P values. ZP3 expression was not associated with response in any of the three cohorts (P=0.90, P=0.86 and P=0.35 respectively), and bootstrap confidence intervals confirmed the approximate intervals. Second, it shows that the null cohorts are informative rather than merely underpowered: in IMvigor210 the rank-biserial r is −0.015 (95% CI −0.17 to 0.14), a TOST equivalence test against a small-effect bound of |r|=0.15 is borderline (P=0.045), and the minimum detectable effect at 80% power is |r|=0.22. Third, it resolves an apparent single positive report: in GSE91061 an apparent association (P=0.008) was confined to an analysis that included on-treatment samples (105 samples from 62 patients, with repeated measurements per patient) and disappeared when the analysis was restricted to pre-treatment samples (P=0.35), under logistic regression with or without immune-feature adjustment (P=0.45–0.58; Firth-penalized P=0.61–0.83), across molecular subtypes, and at the patient level (P=0.18), while pre-specified positive controls (TMB, P=1.1×10⁻⁷; PD-L1 immunohistochemistry, P=1.2×10⁻³) confirm that the analytical pipeline can detect known signals in IMvigor210.

For clinicians and biomarker developers, these results caution against using ZP3 as a stand-alone guide to ICB treatment decisions. For the field, the study offers a reporting template: effect sizes with intervals, equivalence bounds, minimum detectable effects, and internal positive controls, all of which make null results interpretable. A boundary of this study is that bulk transcript abundance does not measure cell-surface ZP3 protein or receptor engagement, and that the single-arm cohorts allow association-level inference rather than formal predictive (treatment-interaction) value; the findings therefore address the proposed axis only at the transcript-association level.

Cancers is an appropriate venue because the journal regularly publishes transcriptomic biomarker evaluations in immunotherapy, including multi-cohort studies of immune-context markers and their predictive value. The present work complements this literature by demonstrating, with a reproducible analytical protocol, when a candidate marker should not be used.

We confirm that this manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, that all authors have approved the submission, and that the authors declare no competing interests. Analysis scripts and frozen result tables will be made available in a public repository upon acceptance. AI-assisted language editing was used during manuscript preparation and is disclosed in the manuscript.
